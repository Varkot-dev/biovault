What you are watching

Each lab computes its count inside its own tenant, where row-level security restricts the scan to that lab's rows. The true number is shown here only because this is a diagram — in the real system it exists for microseconds inside one database session and is never returned, logged, or transmitted.

What crosses the boundary is a single noised integer per lab. That is the entire payload. No record, no identifier, no exact count. Sanger is often withheld entirely: cohorts at or below the threshold are suppressed, because noise cannot hide the difference between nobody and somebody.

Why the total is not the sum you expect

The federated answer is deliberately imprecise, and the interval says by how much. A total that landed exactly on the truth every time would mean the noise was doing nothing. The interval is wide because the count receives only half of each query's ε — the suppression decision is a privacy release of its own and pays for itself out of the same budget.

Each run costs ε × 3, not ε: one release per lab, and the requester sees all three. At the default budget that buys three queries, ever, per 30-day window. Precision and privacy trade directly against each other and the budget caps how many times you can make the trade.
